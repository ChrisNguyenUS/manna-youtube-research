## Macro frames (4)

### ★ HEADLINE — "Data-driven growth that stays creative"
- **They say:** growth is creativity and consistency — post more, be authentic, "you are the niche."
- **You say:** use data to decide *what* to make (outliers, watchlists, your own numbers) and creativity to make it. That's the goldilocks; either one alone loses.
- **Because:** the algorithm rewards what's already proven; outliers are the only honest signal of demand; you ran this workflow to ~450K YouTube subs and 111K IG in months.
- **Supporting takes:** outliers, not averages, tell you what to make next · study the top 50 in your niche before you script · a watchlist beats a mood board · "trial reels" are guessing with extra steps · AI is a research engine, not a writer.
- **Freshness:** contested on the words, fresh on the proof — six channels entered AI × content in 60 days, all tool-first; 2 of 118 competitor videos show the creator's own numbers. Yours if the receipts stay on screen.

### Frame 2 — "Application content beats education"
- **They say:** educational content wins; list the tips, give the framework.
- **You say:** education has split into eduslop, tactical education and *application content* — and application (scenarios, live builds, case studies, showing the skill in the video) is where authority now comes from. Eduslop has stopped working.
- **Because:** copy culture and AI made listed tips free; the only thing that can't be copied is watching you do it. Your "Application Content" reel: 195K views, 7.3×, 5,374 comments.
- **Supporting takes:** a live build outranks a listicle on trust · "comment for the doc" converts better after a demonstration · case studies are the format, not a topic · your own numbers are the only proof copy culture can't fake.
- **Freshness:** fresh — no Ring 1–2 competitor argues this; 7 channels use case studies but on other people's videos, and 0 of 118 competitor videos are a live build on the creator's own channel.

### Frame 3 — "Aim smaller to grow bigger"
- **They say:** go broad for reach; "you are the niche" (rico.incarnati already fights this one — ally, not rival).
- **You say:** one viral Ring-5 video teaches the algorithm the wrong audience; speak to one buyer and the algorithm expands you outward itself. Views from the wrong people are a cost.
- **Because:** 367 short-form videos — the narrow ones converted, the broad ones just got views; the "Viral Trap" and "Rich vs Broke viewers" reels are this frame in the wild.
- **Supporting takes:** the bullseye is the strategy, not the follower count · a 30K-buyer audience out-earns a 1M-scroller audience · "attract rich viewers, not broke viewers."
- **Freshness:** contested — iamjadenly's "cult 10,000" and rico's "steakhouse" are adjacent; yours is the only version with a mechanism (algorithm learning from who engages).

### Frame 4 — "Storytelling is a ladder, not a talent"
- **They say:** you're either a natural storyteller or you're not; storytelling is for entertainers.
- **You say:** storytelling is a six-level ladder (Reporter → Illusionist → Champion → Architect → Translator → Maestro) any business owner can climb with a process — and it's the only way to make boring topics addictive.
- **Because:** the Story Ladder reel (127K, 6.5×, 1,750 comments); Comprehension Mapping, Story Lens, "level 10 story with a level 1 topic" all ladder up.
- **Supporting takes:** stakes make a viewer feel it's about them · loops and arcs are pacing, not fluff · comprehension is a storytelling skill.
- **Freshness:** **fresh, on the data** — v1 called this contested; the deep pass shows only 4 storytelling videos across all 17 competitors, none a worked framework. This is your sole-occupant lane. orate.x's "structure" line is the only neighbor.
